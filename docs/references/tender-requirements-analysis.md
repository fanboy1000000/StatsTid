# Two state tenders measured against StatsTid: a requirements gap analysis

**Date:** 2026-10-26. **Measured against:** `master` at `67809348`.

**Method:** five research passes, one per theme (`general-purpose`, Opus 5.5). Two independent verification passes then re-checked every Met and Partly rating against the code and hunted for missed features behind every Not met (Opus 5.5). A third independent pass traced the product defects the research surfaced. A mechanical check confirmed that all 215 requirement ids appear exactly once.

**Status:** verified, row by row. Where the verification changed a rating or corrected a text, that is marked in the row, and the verification governs.

**Purpose:** an **overview**, at the owner's request (2026-10-26): "a systematic analysis on whether we live up to the requirements described in them. Thereby not saying we should, but I want the overview." Nothing here is a plan or a commitment to build.

## What was measured

The owner provided two real Danish state-sector tenders for a time-and-absence system. Each is the customer's requirements annex (*Bilag 1, Kundens opgavebeskrivelse*), procured as a mini-tender under a public dynamic purchasing system for standard software (*dynamisk indkøbssystem* 02.06, the Human Capital Management area). Both are **historical**: their go-live dates (1 January 2024 and 1 January 2026) have passed. The files sit in the local folder `Tidligere udbud/`, which is not committed. Rows below cite each requirement by the tender's own id.

| | **EM**: Erhvervsministeriet (the Ministry of Industry, Business and Financial Affairs) | **VD**: Vejdirektoratet (the Danish Road Directorate) |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer | The department and 7 agencies, about 3,000 employees | One agency, about 960 employees, with shift workers in Aalborg |
| Replacing | mTIME | A tailored SAP, plus a small shift-planning tool (MINVAGTPLAN) |
| Scope | Time and absence registration, approval, vacation, reporting, payroll keying slips, and deep integration with Navision Stat (the state finance system) | The same core plus **shift planning** (*vagtplanlægning*) and Outlook integration, and an SLA (98.8 % availability, penalties) |
| Requirement types | All mandatory: the tender says "no reservations; all must be met" | **MK** (*mindstekrav*, pass/fail minimums), **K** (*krav*, scored as fully, partly or not met) and **EK** (*evalueringskrav*, scored on quality, with "weighted positively" extras) |
| Requirements | 106 rows: A1–A38 functional, B1–B32 integration, C1–C12 support, D/E implementation and training, plus general and contract clauses | 109 rows: 1–89 (number 19 does not exist in the source), plus general and SLA/contract clauses |

**Measured as a product an institution could buy, StatsTid would fail both tenders.**
- EM makes every requirement mandatory.
- VD has six must-pass minimums, and StatsTid fails all six:
  - FRAV reporting (VD 1) and the Navision Stat/GIS integration (VD 4) are Not met.
  - Single sign-on (VD 6) is Not met.
  - The SLS payroll export (VD 2), the Statens HR exchange (VD 3) and data extraction (VD 5) are only Partly.

That is the expected result for a learning project. Its effort has gone into the calculation core, the approval flow and the audit trail. A tender measures a whole commercial product plus the supplier behind it.

**The single Met** is EM A36, "the system language is Danish".

### What the picture shows

1. **The core works, but Partly is the most common honest answer.** An employee can register work, absence and project hours and send the month. A leader can approve it, and HR can reopen it. Vacation accrues correctly, and the year-end and leaver settlements are computed. The ratings stop at Partly for three recurring reasons:
   - the backend can do it, but there is no screen (HR acting on an employee's behalf);
   - an admin can edit it, but the edit does not reach pay (agreement configuration);
   - the data is right, but it only goes to a stand-in for the real state system (payroll).
2. **Six whole product areas are absent.**
   - **The state's shared systems:** Navision Stat via GIS (41 requirements across the two tenders), FRAV, LDV (the state data warehouse), Statens HR, and a real SLS connection.
   - **Shift planning and Outlook:** VD only, 14 requirements.
   - **Reporting:** statistics, data extracts, Excel/PDF and print.
   - **Notifications:** no e-mail or alert of any kind.
   - **Employee requests:** for example, "transfer my vacation".
   - **The platform:** SSO, an app, backup and restore, rollback, uptime measurement, a user manual, data import, and GDPR deletion.
3. **Auditability is the strongest match.** Both tenders ask for a transaction trail that satisfies Rigsrevisionen (the National Audit Office), and for compliance with Økonomistyrelsen's (ØS, the Agency for Public Finance and Management) guide for payment-derived systems (*Løsningsarkitektur for betalingsafledende systemer*). StatsTid's immutable event history, locked approved months and one-record-per-paid-month design line up with this. The rows stay Partly because of presentation and protection gaps (no log export; hours not in the audit view), and because of the segregation-of-duties defects below.
4. **About a quarter of each tender is not software at all** (54 rows): support hours and response times, a 98.8 % availability target with penalties, training plans, hosting at Statens IT, and a supplier's information-security management system. No code meets these. Where such a clause hides a software capability, such as rollback, backup or a test environment, the row rates that capability.

## Where StatsTid's design deliberately differs from the tenders

These are not oversights. They are earlier decisions that a tender evaluator would score as gaps. Each would need revisiting only if the tenders' model were adopted.

- **A leader cannot see a month's figures until the employee sends it** (owner ruling, S124). The tenders expect a leader to see team registrations at any time.
- **A month cannot be reopened once it has been sent to payroll.** Later changes go through retroactive corrections (ADR-034). The tenders expect admins to reopen approved registrations freely.
- **HR records the vacation-transfer agreement, with no manager-approval step** (ADR-033 D8). Both tenders want the manager's approval documented in the system.
- **Advance vacation** (*forskudsferie*) **is an automatic allowance, not a grant.** The manager's approval of the month counts as the agreement.
- **StatsTid holds little personal data on purpose.** It has no CPR number, no payroll number and no children's data, and keeps a birth date only for the senior-day age gate (ADR-029). EM A6 asks for all of these, plus custom fields.
- **One shared, multi-tenant deployment** (ADR-025). EM wants hosting at Statens IT plus two separate local installations, and a test environment per institution.
- **Deletion versus an immutable history.** Event sourcing never changes a recorded event, so GDPR deletion is designed as nulling personal fields (ADR-025 D3, not built). The ØS guide itself allows deletion only after five years.
- **"Money stays out; SLS owns the kroner"** (ADR-033). This fits the tenders' picture of SLS. But StatsTid sends ordinary working hours to payroll, while the tenders ask for one-off pay elements (*engangsløndele*) and keying slips.

## Defects found in StatsTid along the way

To rate requirements honestly, the research read the code closely, and it found eight defects in what is already built. An independent trace then tried to refute each one first. Five were confirmed outright, two were confirmed and found wider than first reported, and one was partly confirmed. All eight are now in the registers as **REGISTERED**: recorded, not yet adjudicated or fixed, with the trace's proposed severity.

Every trace is a static reading. Docker is unavailable, so nothing was run. No test covers any of them, and each needs a RED-first test before it is fixed.

| # | Register | Severity | What is wrong, in plain terms |
|---|---|---|---|
| D7 | **QUAL-234** | **Critical** | **Clock times entered in Skema never reach payroll or the server's rest check.** For all work registered through the main screen, no evening, night, weekend or holiday supplement is ever computed, and the 11-hour rest check silently skips every day, so a manager sees "no breach". It bears directly on S147: the new supplement window in ADR-043 changes nothing for Skema users unless clock times reach payroll. |
| D1 | **SEC-053** | High | **One HR user can sign a month twice:** they send it for the employee, then approve it as manager. The check stops people approving only their *own* month. Both tenders ask for this four-eyes rule, as does the ØS guide. |
| D3 | **QUAL-230** | High | **A leaver's final month hits a dead end.** The system deactivates leavers automatically the day after their last day. After that, nobody can approve, reject or reopen their month, and payroll's export refuses them too, so the final month cannot be paid through the normal path. |
| D4 | **QUAL-231** | High | **A sick day costs flex in payroll while Skema shows it as neutral.** Skema and the calculation engine spell the sickness codes differently, so the engine never credits the day against the norm. |
| D5 | **QUAL-232** | High | **On-call standby hours are paid twice:** once at the reduced on-call rate, and again as full normal hours with overtime, flex and supplements. Reachable today only through an old API, not a screen. |
| D6 | **QUAL-233** | High | **The admin agreement editor does not change pay.** Payroll reads agreement settings compiled into the code, not the database the editor writes, which contradicts ADR-014 (still marked approved). Wage-type mapping edits are the exception. |
| D8 | **QUAL-235** | High | ***Særlige feriedage*** **(special holiday days) can be earned but never registered as taken.** No screen offers them, so employees spend ordinary vacation instead. The year-end settlement, switched off today, would value every earned day as untaken. |
| D2 | **SEC-054** | Medium | **A GlobalAdmin can send hand-written pay lines to payroll with no approved month and no second person,** through two low-level routes that nothing in the product calls. This is documented as "by design" but was never weighed against segregation of duties. |

The traces with full code paths and scenarios are in the register rows. Two existing documents now read wrong in the light of these:
- QUAL-227's and ADR-043's "paid today" examples assume Skema work earns supplements (QUAL-234).
- ADR-043 F-5(a) says local profiles already reach the engine (QUAL-233).

## Where the tenders themselves are ambiguous

- **EM A7** "*eksakte værdier*" may mean exact hours and minutes rather than live balances.
- **The tenders' "HR-administrator"** does not map cleanly onto StatsTid's roles:
  - "Lokal HR" cannot grant roles; "Lokal administrator" and above can.
- **VD's numbering and types:**
  - VD 56 is printed K, but its text says *evalueringskrav*.
  - VD 79 has no type printed.
  - VD 19 does not exist.
- **VD 45 and VD 47** refer to annexes 11–13, which we do not have.
- **VD 21** "*fasttidsdage*" matches no state entitlement we could find.
- **VD 26** "*sætter stedfortræder på … regler*" most plausibly means substitutes for people.
- **VD 58 versus VD 2 / EM B2** describe two payroll models: automatic transfer of everything that affects fixed pay, versus a manual export of one-off elements.
- **VD 7** is a scored requirement to host at Statens IT, while §3 only "prefers" it.
- **VD §7's** penalty example uses a 1-hour response time, while its table says 2 hours.
- **EM A38 and EM §8.3** are the same requirement twice.
- **EM-G7** ("same functionality as mTIME") cannot be measured: the tender gives no mTIME specification.

## How to read the tables

- **One primary row per requirement id**, with the tender's id: `EM A1`, `VD 8`.
  - Synthetic ids cover unnumbered general statements (`EM-G1` …, `VD-G1` …) and contract sections (`VD §5.7` …).
  - Composite requirements may have sub-rows, such as `EM A1 (a)`, when their parts rate differently. The tallies count primary rows only.
- **Type:** EM rows are `Must`. VD rows carry the printed type (MK, K or EK), or "Untyped" where the source prints none.
- **Evidence** cites code as it stood at `67809348` (`path:line`, test classes, docs). Line numbers drift as the code changes; the symbol names are the durable reference.
- **Verification marks.** A row the verification changed carries **Verification: rated X → Y.** in its Gap column. A row whose rating stood but whose text was corrected or extended carries **Verification:**.
- **Link** points to the ROADMAP item, register row or ADR that already covers the gap. A link means the gap is known, not that work is planned.

#### What this theme tells us

- **The everyday loop is strong; the "back office" around it is thin.** An employee can register work, absence and project hours and send the month in one click; a leader sees the team, opens each person's month and approves (singly or in bulk). That core is well built and tested. What the tenders also expect — statistics, data extracts, Excel/PDF, print, comments on a registration, project administrators — does not exist at all. Those six requirements (EM A23–A26, A30, A35; VD 29, 30, 37) are the clearest gaps.
- **HR can do much more through the API than on screen.** The backend lets HR (and local admins) register for another employee, send a month for them, approve, reopen and read any in-scope employee's balances. But every screen that registers or shows balances works only for the person logged in, and the leader overview lists only the people you are the designated approver for. So "HR acts on behalf of an employee", which both tenders need (maternity leave, sickness), has no screen. The HR "Opfølgning" page even sends HR to the team overview, where those employees may not appear.
- **The four-eyes rule is half-enforced.** Nobody can manager-approve their *own* month, and a test proves it. But the tender's wording is broader: one person must not approve a month both as the employee and as the chef. In StatsTid an HR admin can send (employee-approve) a month on someone's behalf and then manager-approve that same month; the system records who sent it but never compares the two. This is the most important correctness finding in this theme. It is cross-referenced to R5 for VD 35, which covers four-eyes on payroll-affecting actions. An independent trace confirmed it; it is registered as SEC-053.
- **Leavers may hit a dead end.** S138 fixed *sending* a deactivated leaver's final month. Approve, reject and reopen still look up the employee as "active only", and the audit step throws if the employee is inactive. Read from the code alone, a leaver's final month apparently cannot reach "approved", which is the state payroll export needs. An independent trace confirmed it and found it wider: the calculating payroll export also refuses a deactivated leaver, so the final month cannot be paid through the normal path (registered as QUAL-230).
- **Where StatsTid differs from the tenders on purpose:** a leader cannot see a month's figures until the employee sends it (owner ruling S124), and a month cannot be reopened once sent to payroll — later changes go through retroactive corrections (ADR-034). Both are defensible, but a tender evaluator could read them as "partly". The tenders' "HR-administrator" also does not map cleanly onto StatsTid's roles: "Lokal HR" cannot grant roles, only "Lokal administrator" and above can.
- **The tenders are ambiguous in places.** "*Eksakte værdier*" (EM A7) may mean exact hours and minutes rather than live balances. For the VD evaluation requirements (*evalueringskrav*), I rated the core requirement and listed the positively weighted extras as gaps, so a Partly there can mean "core met".

#### What this theme tells us

- **The calculation core is real but not yet right for the state.** StatsTid has a deterministic, versioned rule engine covering norm, flex, overtime, *merarbejde*, supplements, on-call and rest periods, plus dated per-employee history. But the team's own verified research (S146) found the overtime/flex model wrong in kind (weekly instead of monthly norm, automatic instead of ordered overtime, the wrong supplement table). So every "state working-time rules" requirement (EM A8, VD 11, VD 12) is honestly Partly until the S147 model program (ADR-043) lands.
- **Configuration screens exist, but most of what they configure does not reach pay.** The admin agreement editor writes to the database, but payroll's calculation reads the agreement tables compiled into the code (the code itself says "agreement configs are code"). Local agreement profiles only split a month and never change a figure (QUAL-204). This is the biggest gap behind EM A16, VD 20 and VD 26 (registered as QUAL-233). Two kinds of edit are exceptions: wage-type mappings do reach the export, and entitlement quotas drive booking and settlement. A customer-configurable product needs the database config to be the one payroll uses, dated, and frozen for replay. Today the architecture document (ADR-014) and the code disagree on this.
- **Two defects surfaced by this analysis, since confirmed by an independent trace and registered (QUAL-231, QUAL-232).** (1) A sick day registered in Skema gives no norm credit in payroll's flex, because the engine's credit list spells child-sick types differently and omits `SICK_DAY`, so paid flex drops while the screen shows zero. (2) Hours registered as on-call standby count as fully worked hours for flex and overtime, on top of the reduced on-call rate. Each needs a RED-first test before it is fixed. The verification found a third, larger one in the same area: Skema's clock times never reach payroll, so no unsocial-hours supplement is computed for Skema work at all (QUAL-234, Critical).
- **Shift planning (VD 46-57, 59, 61) and Outlook are absent, not partial.** StatsTid has no shift, rota, planner role or calendar integration. The shift-planning parts of the overview rows rated by R1 are also absent: VD-G1's shift scope, VD 8's *vagtplanlæggere* and *vagtplansbrugere* roles, and VD 9's overview of the shift plan and of own shifts against registrations. The only relevant building block is the rest-period checker, which works on registered hours after the fact. VD 53 was rated Partly for it; the verification moved it to Not met, because there is no plan to check, and for Skema work the server check gets no clock times at all (QUAL-234). More importantly, the verified research says the state's unsocial-hours supplement applies to work "ordered or on an approved duty roster" (*godkendt tjenestefordeling*). So a planned roster is a prerequisite for paying supplements correctly, and for VD 25 (keep the night supplement when sick), not just a planning convenience.
- **Master data is thin by design choice, not by accident.** StatsTid deliberately holds little personal data: no CPR number, payroll number or children's data (date of birth was added only for the senior-day age gate, ADR-029). EM A6 asks for those fields and for custom fields. Children's birthdays would also be needed to grant *omsorgsdage* per child rather than flat (VD 21).
- **Tender ambiguities.** "*Fasttidsdage*" (VD 21) is not a term we could match to any state entitlement. VD 45 refers to annexes 12/13 that we do not have. VD 26's "*sætter stedfortræder på … regler*" (substitutes on rules) most plausibly means substitutes for people and was rated that way. VD 56 is printed K but its text says evaluation requirement.

#### What this theme tells us

- **The calculation core is strong; the workflow around it is thin.** StatsTid's vacation engine goes well beyond what these tenders spell out. It accrues 2,08 days a month (*samtidighedsferie*). It keeps special days on their own calendar-year timeline. It caps advance vacation. And at year end it splits leftover days into their legal buckets: transfer (§21), payout (§24), forfeiture (§34, never automatic), an impediment such as sickness (§22), and termination (§26). It is research-backed and heavily tested. What the tenders mostly ask for, though, is the human layer: requests, manager approval, forms, extracts, alerts. That layer is largely missing. The verification also found that *særlige feriedage* accrue but cannot be registered as taken on any screen (QUAL-235).
- **No notifications of any kind exist: no mail, no in-app alerts, no configurable "agents."** EM A15 and VD 22 are greenfield. The HR "Opfølgning" (follow-up) page is the natural data source for many of the triggers, but HR has to open it; nothing pushes. Two of the example triggers rest on concepts StatsTid does not model at all: vacation without pay, and sickness that qualifies for sickness-benefit reimbursement (*sygedagpengerefusion*).
- **The year-end close is built but switched off, and its payouts cannot reach payroll.** The automated close is dormant until an operator sets a go-live date (`Settlement:GoLiveDate`). Special-day payout needs a second flag. Every settlement payroll line carries a placeholder pay code (`SLS_TBD_*`) that the export guard deliberately blocks until the real codes are agreed with SLS (the state payroll system). So "payout at year end" (VD 14) is computed correctly but not deliverable. This was a deliberate safety posture, not an oversight.
- **Several requirements conflict with deliberate design choices.** (1) The §21 transfer agreement is recorded by HR, with no manager-approval step (ADR-033 D8 says so explicitly). Both tenders want the manager's approval documented in the system. (2) Advance vacation is an automatic allowance rather than a "grant": manager approval of the month counts as the agreement. (3) The 16-month holiday-taking period is modelled only as far as settlement timing needs it, so a true 16-month forecast (EM A11 / VD 16) needs domain modelling, not just a new screen.
- **There is no "request" concept.** The only approval workflow is the monthly sign-off (submit, approve/reject, reopen), and that part fully meets the "reject → reopen the month" clause. Employee-initiated requests with approval, history and HR notification (EM A14 / VD 24) would be a new domain object, not an extension of an existing one.
- **Ambiguities in the tenders.** "Reopen the employee's registrations for the current month" (EM A14) could mean StatsTid's reject flow, which returns the month to the employee, or its reopen flow (approved months only). Both exist. VD 17's "visual overview of departments' vacation, approved/not approved" mixes a liability extract with a team vacation calendar; StatsTid has neither as such.

### R4 — Integrations with the state's shared systems (ØS: SLS/HR-Løn, FRAV, Statens HR, Navision Stat/GIS, LDV, API, case systems)

**What StatsTid's payroll integration actually is today (the baseline every row below refers to).** The Payroll service (`src/Integrations/StatsTid.Integrations.Payroll/Program.cs`) has one approval-gated route, `POST /api/payroll/calculate-and-export` (`:324`, policy `LocalAdminOrAbove` `:602`). It handles **one employee and one whole calendar month per call** (`RefusePeriodNotAMonth` `:377`), and only if that month is APPROVED (`:380`) and the previous month is approved too (`PriorMonthToRecheck` `:524`; test `PayrollHostWeeklyTests.CalculateAndExport_October_SeptemberNotApproved_Returns409_NamesSeptember_WritesNothing`). It maps rule-engine lines to wage types through the dated `wage_type_mappings` table, which has an admin screen (`WageTypeMappingEndpoints.cs`, route `global/loenartstilknytning`, `App.tsx:160`). It then writes, in one transaction, a per-(employee, month) lock/idempotency record with an audit row (ADR-034; `PayrollExportRecordTests`). After commit it **POSTs JSON to `mock-payroll`** (`PayrollExportService.cs:55, :366`), a test double that keeps payloads in an in-memory list (`docker/mock-payroll/Program.cs`; SEC-018 "mock services stand in for external systems"). No background dispatcher exists, so the lock means "handed off", not "delivered" (ADR-034 D2). The wage-type codes are internal `SLS_0xxx` codes whose match to the real SLS *løndel* catalogue is unverified (`docs/references/danish-agreements.md:203-210`); settlement lines use `SLS_TBD_*` placeholders that the outbound guard refuses (`init.sql:4253`). The pipe-delimited `SlsExportFormatter` is a self-designed layout: its `Format` is called only by tests, and `FormatCorrections` is called by `/api/payroll/export-corrections` (`Program.cs:768`). No screen triggers an export: the HR list "Godkendt, ikke eksporteret" says *"Eksport til løn sker i dag via Lønadministrationens API — ingen skærm kalder den endnu"* (`FollowUpLists.tsx:218`). The two raw routes `/export` and `/export-period` (`:213`, `:265`, GlobalAdminOnly) skip approval by design and take a single employee's profile. The External service (`src/Integrations/StatsTid.Integrations.External`) forwards a caller-supplied JSON object to `mock-external` (`ExternalApiClient.cs:18, :29`). Its outbox poller reads `destination='external'` (`EventConsumerService.cs:98`), but nothing in `src` writes such envelopes.

#### What this theme tells us

- **The one real asset is the payroll export, and it is solid but has no real receiver.** Approved months become wage-type lines, one employee and one month at a time. The export refuses unapproved months and requires the previous month to be approved. Each paid month is recorded once: a second identical export does nothing, and a different one is refused and must go through a correction. Every export is audited. What is missing is the last mile: the lines go to a stand-in "mock payroll" service that just keeps them in memory, the codes (`SLS_0110` …) are internal and not checked against SLS's real *løndel* list, nobody can start an export from a screen, and there is no "export the whole unit" button. Both tenders' payroll MKs (must-pass requirements) would therefore fail on "directly to SLS" today. Connecting to the real SLS web service also needs onboarding with ØS, which is partly an operational step, not only code.
- **Navision Stat via GIS (ØS's standard integration interface) is a whole missing product area.** It accounts for 28 of EM's 34 ids here (B3–B30) and 13 of VD's 23 (VD 4, 39, 44, 62–71), plus the Navision legs of both umbrella rows. StatsTid has one flat list of project codes per organisation that people register hours against. The tenders assume a multi-dimensional "registration frame" (dimensions, aliases, cases and case tasks) imported from Navision. They also expect approved hours posted back to Navision's journals with batch numbers, voucher numbers, reconciliation and reversal postings. None of that exists; the nearest things are the project list, the leader's monthly approval and the event-sourced history of changes.
- **FRAV (the state's absence statistics), LDV (the state data warehouse) and Statens HR have nothing specific.** Absence is recorded in detail, but nothing reports it to FRAV in ØS's format, nothing feeds LDV daily, and there is no Statens HR connector. There is only a bulk import for manager relations and one-at-a-time user creation through the API.
- **The API is real but built for StatsTid's own screens, not for data extraction.** It has a typed, CI-checked OpenAPI description of 113 paths. Its reads are per employee or per screen, and logging in means a person's username and password. A SAS job or a case system would need dataset- or extract-style endpoints and machine credentials, which do not exist.
- **Design fit and tension.** StatsTid's rule "money stays out; SLS owns the kroner" (ADR-033) fits how the tenders picture SLS. But StatsTid also sends normal working hours to payroll, while the tenders ask only for *engangsløndele* (one-off pay elements). Of the three *tastebilag* (keying slip) categories, holiday-pay adjustment (løndel 5062) is documented but never produced, and "vacation without pay" is not modelled; StatsTid's "leave without pay" is a different thing. The unsocial-hours supplement rates are already registered as wrong against the state rules (QUAL-227, in the S147 programme), so any keying slip built today would carry wrong figures.
- **Ambiguity in the tenders.** VD 58 asks for *automatic* transfer of approved registrations "that can affect fixed pay", while VD 2 / EM B2 ask for a manual-style export of one-off elements. These are two different models, and the tenders do not say which data belongs to which. VD-G4's "reporting of vacation and absence per the circular" most likely means FRAV (circular 2022/9247); I rated it that way.

#### What this theme tells us

- **Most of this theme is the supplier, not the software.** 53 of 85 rows are support, SLA, training, hosting or contract obligations that no code can meet. Where a software capability sits inside them, StatsTid usually lacks it. The missing pieces: single sign-on (SSO), an app, backup and restore, rollback to a previous version, a hosted test environment, uptime measurement, active-user counting, a user manual, data import and export, and GDPR deletion. The project has put its effort into domain correctness and audit depth, not into platform operations. That matches its stated status: a learning project, not deployed, with a hardening pass "owed" before production.
- **Auditability is the strength, and it lines up with ØS's guide.** The guide (*Løsningsarkitektur for betalingsafledende systemer*, August 2024) asks for logging, a clear transaction trail and locked approved data. StatsTid has an immutable event store, a request-level audit log, an org-scoped audit view, and one export record per employee-month with a manifest that refuses duplicates. Approved months are locked and can only be reopened explicitly. The gaps are presentation and protection: hours registrations are not in the audit view, there is no export, and logs are append-only by convention, not protected in the database.
- **The most important finding is four-eyes.** StatsTid stops people approving their *own* month. It does not stop one HR admin from approving *both* legs: approving on the employee's behalf, then approving as the manager through the HR fallback. A GlobalAdmin can also export payroll lines through two routes that skip approval "by design". This conflicts with ØS's guide: at least two people must approve before data goes to another system (§5.5), and a privileged user must not complete a payout alone (§4.1). It also conflicts with VD 35 and EM A1's explicit four-eyes sentence. Both paths are confirmed by an independent trace and registered (SEC-053, SEC-054).
- **Two of StatsTid's design choices meet the tenders head-on.**
  - **Deployment:** ADR-025 assumes one shared multi-tenant deployment. EM wants hosting at Statens IT plus separate local installations at two institutions, and a test environment per institution.
  - **GDPR deletion:** this collides with event sourcing (events are never changed) and with the 5-year accounting retention. ADR-025 D3 designs nulling out personal fields instead of deleting rows, and the ØS guide itself only allows deletion after 5 years.
  - StatsTid stores no CPR numbers or bank details, and sends payroll *quantities*, not money. That shrinks the payment-data scope those guides worry about.
- **Where the tenders themselves are ambiguous:**
  - VD 7 is a scored requirement to host at SIT, while §3 says the customer only "prefers" it.
  - VD 79 has no type printed.
  - VD §7's penalty example uses a 1-hour response time, while the table says 2 hours.
  - EM A38 and EM §8.3 are the same requirement twice.
  - EM-G7 ("same functionality as mTIME") cannot be measured: the tender gives no mTIME specification.
  - Both go-live dates (1 Jan 2024 and 1 Jan 2026) have passed: these are historical tenders.

## Method and verification

**How the analysis was built.**
1. **Requirement inventory.** Every requirement in both documents was given one id: the tender's own id where it has one, and a synthetic id for unnumbered general statements and contract sections. Each id was assigned to exactly one of five themes, 215 ids in all.
2. **Research, one pass per theme** (`general-purpose`, Opus 5.5, read-only, in parallel). Each pass read the exact tender text and then the code, not the product spec: `SYSTEM_TARGET.md` describes the design target, not what is built. The four ratings were defined in advance:
   - *Met* needs the whole requirement working, with a screen or documented API where it is user-facing.
   - *Partly* needs a substantive part, and the gap must be named.
   - *Not met* needs an adversarial search, in Danish and English, before it is written.
   - *Outside software* is rated on any software capability hidden inside the clause.
   - When in doubt, a pass picks the lower rating.
3. **Mechanical completeness check.** Every id appears as exactly one primary row with one of the four ratings. The check ran on the research output and again on the corrected tables.
4. **Independent verification, three passes** (Opus 5.5; the usual Fable review lens was at its usage limit, and the owner had directed Opus for the interim):
   - **V1** re-checked R1–R3: 102 rows, 99 confirmed, 3 downgraded.
   - **V2** re-checked R4–R5: 151 rows, 145 confirmed, 5 downgraded, 1 reclassified.
   - Both opened the cited evidence for every Met and Partly row, ran a counter-search for every Not met, and spot-checked about 110 citations. Only two pointed at the wrong line.
   - **V3** traced the eight suspected product defects, trying to refute each first: 7 confirmed (two of them wider than reported) and 1 partly confirmed.
5. **Corrections applied.** Where the verification differs, it governs. Each changed row says so in its Gap column.
