## Plain language — why

**The rule.** "Four-eyes" means two different people stand behind every paid month: the person who *sends* the month (the employee, or HR on the employee's behalf) and the person who *approves* it must differ, and nobody approves their own month. The Backend enforces this when a month is approved. Payroll, the last place an approved month turns into money, checks it again rather than trusting every path that ever wrote "APPROVED" (S147, SEC-053).

**What is wrong today (QUAL-246).** Payroll's check runs only at the very end, inside the export's database transaction. By then the month has already been calculated and its *manifest* (the stored proof of a calculation, ADR-016 D10) written. And when the check refuses, it answers with the generic `payroll-export-conflict`, the same answer as "this month was already exported with other content" or "the month changed under you". A caller, and the HR export screen planned as HRP-022, cannot tell "a second person must approve this month" from those, so it cannot show the remedy.

**The change (refinement tidy 12b, option (a), P-5).** *Admission* (payroll's first checks, before it calculates anything) already reads the month's approval row to see that it is APPROVED. It now also checks the two equalities, approver = sender and approver = employee, and refuses with a 409 of its own kind, **`payroll-four-eyes`**, carrying no date. Nothing is calculated or written. The existing check inside the export transaction stays, because the approval row could change between admission and export (a race); it answers the same kind.

**What was given up.** Only option (b)'s smaller diff (renaming the kind at the export site alone). Under (b) the month would still be calculated first, leaving a proof for a month that cannot be paid. *Why it matters to a PM:* a refusal that names its cause tells the operator what to do next.

The segregation-of-duties rule — *nobody approves their own period* — has **no single enforcement
point**. It is re-stated by hand wherever someone remembered:

- `ApprovalPeriodRepository.QueryUnitLeaderApproverCandidatesAsync`: `ul.user_id <> @employeeId`
  and `mv.vikar_user_id <> @employeeId`
- `DesignatedApproverAuthorizer.QueryUnitLeaderKindAsync`: `e.user_id <> @actorId`
