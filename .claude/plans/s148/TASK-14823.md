## Plain language — why

**The problem.** Agreements are often signed after they take effect, so since wave 4 a GlobalAdmin can publish an agreement configuration "from" a past Monday, and change a position override from a past date; an institution's administrator can date its local profile into the past too. Payroll has already paid some of the months those dates reach. Until now nothing told anyone: the paid month stayed paid on the old values, silently. You ruled (Q-2 = (a)) that every paid month such a write reaches goes on HR's correction worklist, where a GlobalAdmin sees it and recalculates. Nothing is recalculated automatically.

**Which months.** A write reaches the days from its date on which its new version is the one in force, until its OK version ends (an OK24 version stops applying on 31 March 2026). An employee is listed for a paid month when, on some day that month pays for and inside that reach, the employee matched the write on that same day: the right agreement code for an agreement configuration; that and the right position for a position override; that and the right institution, by the dated organisation, for a local profile. A month "pays for" its own days plus the days of its opening Monday–Sunday week that fall in the previous month (payroll settles each week with the month that holds its Sunday). The same matching stretches also flag the settled holiday years they reach, because a settled year's day counts come from the configured weekly norm; flagged, never revalued.

**Worked example.** The parties sign a change to HK's OK24 values that applies from Monday 5 January 2026. On Tuesday 10 November 2026 a GlobalAdmin publishes it, dated 5 January. Jens is on HK the whole year, and payroll has exported January to May 2026 for him.
- The publish **waits its turn** (the "pay inputs" lock, below), writes the version, and lists Jens's **January, February, March and April** on the worklist, each row with the trigger `CONFIG_PUBLISHED`, effective from 5 January, pointing at the publish's own event. **Not May:** the OK24 version stops applying on 31 March; April is listed only because April's pay settled the week Monday 30 March – Sunday 5 April, which holds two OK24 days (AC-C2g).
- Had the change been a **position override** for position P, and Jens held P only until Sunday 29 March before moving to Q, only January to March would be listed: his matching stretch ends on a Sunday, so no later month paid any of its days (AC-C2j).
- Had it been **STY01's local profile**, and Jens moved from STY01 to STY02 on Monday 30 March, the same: January to March (AC-C2j). A colleague who moved *into* STY01 only after March is not listed for March; one who moved *out* after March is (AC-C2f).
- HR sees each row with its Danish label (TASK-14824). A GlobalAdmin recalculates; from wave 5's engine switch the recalculation pays the new values (TASK-14828-T's AC-C2c pay half). The trigger never blocks the recalculation: a configuration write changes no wage-type key.

**The "pay inputs" lock, and why the order matters.** Payroll calculates a month first and records it afterwards. A change committed in between would find no recording to list, and payroll would then record the month on the old values: paid wrongly, and nobody told. So every writer of a pay input and every payroll recording take turns on one system-wide lock (TASK-14821 put it on the Backend's person-level writers; TASK-14831 puts it on payroll's recording). You put it on the six configuration writes, as their **first** statement. Each of these transactions must also run at **READ COMMITTED**, stated explicitly: only then does the worklist read, made after the wait, see a recording that committed during the wait. The local profile's write runs at REPEATABLE READ today and moves; under it, the wait would hide exactly the recording the write must list.

**One defect you fix on the way (A9).** The local-profile save reads "the profile I am replacing" before it waits for the lock, and computes its audit row and event from that read. If another administrator replaces that same profile while this save waits, this save's version check still passes (a replacement restarts the version at 1), so its audit row and event would describe replacing a row that is no longer there. Today the wait is microseconds; your lock makes it as long as the lock is held, so you close it: when the profile the save locks is not the one it read, the save answers its existing "precondition failed" (412) and writes nothing. A related weakness, a stale version number passing because the version restarts at 1 even with no race, is registered for later, not fixed here: fixing it changes how versions are numbered (ADR-018) and may affect other dated tables too.

**What it costs.** Configuration writes, person-level corrections and payroll recordings now wait for each other system-wide. A large central publish that lists many months holds the lock while it commits, and every payroll recording waits for it; TASK-14823-T measures one, and a slow figure becomes a performance-register row (O-15).

**What does not change.** No route shape, no request or response member, no screen (the labels are TASK-14824's), no pay figure, no recalculation, no new event type. The writes' own validation, refusals and audit rows are unchanged.
