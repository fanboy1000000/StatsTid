## Plain language — why

TASK-14843 changes how the server measures the 11-hour rest. Until now it measured the gap between one working day and the next. From now on it does what the authority does: at the end of each piece of work it looks 24 hours back and takes the **longest unbroken rest** in that stretch; under 11 hours is a finding, **dated the day the work ends**, one per day. It counts real elapsed hours in Danish time, reads two days before the month, and lets the HK/PROSA "derogation" excuse a short rest only down to 8 hours.

The number is shown **rounded down** to one decimal (10 h 59 min shows 10.9, never "11.0 against a minimum of 11.0"), and the engine is handed the Danish time zone by its host rather than looking it up. That moves numbers and dates the old tests pinned, on purpose. **Example:** 22:00–23:59 on Tuesday, then 06:00–14:00 on Wednesday was "6.0 hours, dated Tuesday"; it is now "8.0 hours, dated Wednesday", because in the 24 hours before Wednesday 14:00 the longest unbroken rest is Tuesday 14:00–22:00. **Another:** one 24-hour shift was "no finding"; it is now "0.0 hours, dated the day it ends".

This pass:
- **rewrites the shared table** (`rest-parity-scenarios.json`, read by the engine's C# fact and the screen's vitest) to the new shape, **date plus rest hours**, recomputes all sixteen rows and adds seventeen: the cases the old check missed (a split day, a day shift plus a night shift, a 24-hour shift, 13 hours and 13 hours and one minute), both daylight-saving nights, the read two days back, the 8-hour floor on both sides, five that pin details of RR-1 on both sides, and the adjudicator's row for work typed inside March's missing hour;
- **re-pins every existing fact whose expected value moves**, each one named below with old → new and labelled, so a reviewer sees each move as deliberate (the census, the heart of this brief);
