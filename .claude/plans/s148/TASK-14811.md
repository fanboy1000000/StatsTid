## Plain language — why

**The problem (QUAL-236).** When a child falls ill, a state employee may take paid leave to look after it. StatsTid encodes this wrongly in three ways:

1. **The number of days.** It gives AC staff 1 day, HK staff 2 and PROSA staff 3, in both agreement periods. The agreement texts give **2 days to everyone under OK24**, and from **1 April 2026 (OK26) 3 days plus the rest of the day the child fell ill** (the "call-home day": the parent is called home from work, and the remaining working hours of that day are paid leave).
2. **Per year instead of per illness.** The texts count the days per illness ("per episode"): each new illness starts a new count, and there is no yearly limit. StatsTid counts them against a yearly quota, so a second illness in the same year is refused.
3. **No place for the call-home hours.** There is no code to register them.

**What changes, in one paragraph.** Which child's-sick codes an employee may register now depends on the OK version of the day: under OK24 day 1 and day 2; from 1 April 2026 day 1, 2 and 3 plus the new call-home code, for all five agreements (AC's OK26 rule is agreed but its circular is not issued yet, so it is labelled provisional, and so are its research and teaching variants, which inherit the AC agreement). **Where those answers live:** how many day codes are offered comes from the dated child's-sick entitlement setting in the database (2 under OK24, 3 under OK26), so an administrator who edits that day count changes which day codes the screen offers; whether the call-home day applies comes from a small table in code (OK24 no, OK26 yes), registered as a known shortcut until the setting can carry it. Day 2 and day 3 are the calendar days after day 1, **whether or not they are working days**: an illness that starts on a Saturday has used up its day 2 on Sunday, so the employee registers day 3 on Monday with no weekend rows, because no row is needed on a day with no planned hours. An illness keeps the OK version of its first day, so an illness that starts on 30 March 2026 has 2 days even though 1 April belongs to OK26. The yearly quota goes; the HR opt-in that says whether an employee may use child's sick days at all stays. The balance screen shows the allowance per illness instead of a yearly remainder.

**A worked example.** An HK employee's child falls ill on Saturday 11 April 2026 (OK26: 3 days). Day 1 is Saturday, day 2 Sunday; neither has planned hours, so neither needs a row. On Monday 13 April the employee registers "Barns 3. sygedag": accepted. The same pattern from Saturday 28 March 2026 (OK24: 2 days) has no day 3, so Monday 30 March is refused.

**Why pay changes, and why that is safe.** This is the sprint's one change to the engine's arithmetic: the call-home code is a new paid absence, credited toward the norm like a sick day. The engine's version marker therefore moves once, from `weekly-timeline-v2` to `weekly-timeline-v3`, in the same commit that classifies the new code as credited, so a payroll proof recorded under the old arithmetic is refused at replay instead of silently re-read (ADR-016 D10). No demo payslip changes: the demo writes no child's sick day (`REF:763`), so the pay pin (PIN-1) holds.

**What the call-home hours are paid as.** Payroll needs a wage-type code for every line it exports. No code for the call-home day is known yet; the plan maps it to the child's first sick day's code, `SLS_0530`, shared with `CHILD_SICK_DAY` (as sick leave and parental leave already share `SLS_0540`), and labels that provisional until the payroll experts confirm it (R-16). That is a named interim limit, not a guess hidden in code.

**What is deliberately not built here.** The chain reads only this employee's rows: the other parent's days are not modelled (a registered shortcut, A-16). And no save can remove a registered day yet: the check that refuses "removing day 1 while day 2 exists" becomes reachable only when cell clearing lands (TASK-14840, later in this wave), which extends your chain and owns that test (AC-S0-2j).
