## Plain language — why

Two small sets of tests that S147 deferred (its tidy-pass class D, items 5 and 12a). Both are **regression pins** (kind R): tests that hold on today's code and must keep holding. They guard behaviour that is right today against a later change quietly breaking it. Nothing in production changes.

**1. Særlige feriedage at the edges.** Every employee earns five særlige feriedage a year (the agreement's "6th holiday week"). Since S147 the Skema grid offers a "Særlig feriedag" row only where the employee's agreement has a særlige-feriedage configuration in force. Two edges are not pinned yet:
- **April 2026** is the first month under the new agreement version, OK26 (OK24 ran to 31 March 2026). AC-5b proves that April reads OK26's configuration, not OK24's. An off-by-one-month mistake at an agreement transition is exactly the "version correctness" the domain invariant names.
- **A configuration that ends in the middle of a month** (AC-5a). The criterion says the row is served "only for the days it covers", but the month view decides once per month, on the month's last day: that is the established display convention, and saving a day values it independently, so it is not a defect. The ruling (NR-1 (a)) pins exactly that: a configuration ending mid-month is served for the month before and not for the month it ends in. The fact's name and docstring say so, and the limit (in the ending month the grid offers no row, even on covered days) is registered by the Orchestrator.

**2. The four-eyes rule inside the database.** The person who sent a month for approval cannot also approve it (S147, SEC-053). The rule is enforced twice: by a C# check at the endpoint, and inside the SQL statement that records the approval. The C# check refuses first, so no test ever exercises the SQL's own half for **old ("legacy") rows**, where only `submitted_by` is filled in. AC-12a calls the repository directly, below the endpoint, and proves the SQL alone refuses the sender and admits a different person. This is RES-003's lesson: a rule written in two places needs each copy tested, or one can rot unseen.
