## Plain language — why

**The problem.** Since TASK-14820 (wave 4) the system knows which institution an employee belonged to on each day: a transfer from Styrelse A to Styrelse B is a dated fact, and the shared "who was this person on that day" read returns the right institution for any day. But payroll plans a month before it reads anyone's record, and its plan is cut only where something it knows about changes: the OK version, the agreement code, the part-time fraction or position, the employment window, a dated configuration. **A transfer is not on that list yet,** so payroll would treat June as one piece even if the person moved on the 15th, and would ask only the first institution's local profile for the whole month.

**Why it matters now.** Today the institution changes no pay figure: the rule engine computes with the agreement's built-in values, which do not depend on the institution. Later in this wave (the T14b stack, TASK-14826 … 14829) each institution's own weekly norm and flex cap reach pay, day by day. From then on a month paid as one piece across a transfer would pay the days spent at A under B's rules. This task makes sure payroll already cuts the month at the transfer, so that switch has the right pieces to price.

**What changes, in four parts.**
1. **Payroll reads the employee's transfer dates** with the other two date histories it already reads (profile changes and agreement-code changes), once per calculation, over the same window, through a small reader of its own, `IOrganisationHistoryReader` (A1). Three tasks this wave read the organisation history, each with a different question ("which organisation on this day", "on which dates did it change", "who belonged there over this stretch"), so they share a definition of an organisation row, not code.
2. **A transfer date becomes a cut** through the existing "the employee's record changed" boundary (`EmployeeProfileChange`); there is no new kind of boundary. The same dates cut the opening week's "context days" (the previous month's days of a week this month settles) and the local-profile lookup, so each piece of the month asks its own institution's profile history.
3. **A payroll service built without this reader refuses,** with a fixed message that names the missing registration and the file to fix, exactly as payroll already does for the previous-month approval reader. It never quietly plans the month without transfer cuts.
4. **The payroll host registers the reader.**

**Worked example: a transfer on Monday 15 June 2026.** Mette works at Styrelse A (STY01). On Saturday 20 June HR records that she moved to Styrelse B (STY02) from Monday 15 June. June is not exported yet.
- When payroll calculates June, the plan has **two parts**: 1–14 June and 15–30 June. Both are EMPLOYED; the record names the reason `EmployeeProfileChange` on 15 June (with two parts, both carry that cause: FAIL-007). Each part's employment record is read on its own first day, so the first part names STY01 and the second STY02, and the local-profile lookup asks STY01's history for 1–14 June and STY02's for 15–30 June.
- **What her June payslip shows at this task's merge:** the same amounts as an unsplit June, because until the T14b stack the engine prices with the agreement's built-in values, and her agreement, fraction and position did not change; each line is filed under the part whose days it is dated in. (Whether a per-part rule's lines come out as one line or two per wage type depends on how the engine dates them; that is `(unproven)` and changes no amount.) From the T14b stack on, 1–14 June is priced with STY01's profile and 15–30 June with STY02's (TASK-14828-T's AC-C13a pay half).
- **If June had already been exported** when HR recorded the move, June's exported lines, export record and content hash do not change (AC-C13b): nothing recalculates by itself (ADR-013). TASK-14820's hand-off has put June on HR's worklist; a GlobalAdmin's recalculation then pays it in two parts.
- **A move on a Wednesday is not refused.** Payroll refuses a month only for an agreement-code change in the middle of a Monday–Sunday week (`PeriodCalculationService.cs:3738-3760`), because that changes the wage-type key; a transfer changes no wage-type key (`REF:550`).

**What does not change.** No route, no request or response shape, no screen, no demo payslip (the demo never transfers anyone, so PIN-1 holds: AC-C13c). Replay never plans and never reads the history (ADR-016 D10's replay contract), so it is untouched. The organisation is not yet frozen into the manifest's pieces; that is TASK-14830's (`FrozenPiece.OrgId`).

**What it means for you.** Npgsql 8 maps `DateOnly.MinValue` to PostgreSQL's `-infinity` (the reasoning is written at `UserOrganisationRepository.cs:50-57`), and the two history tables store their "since always" rows differently: an organisation row from the SQL literal `DATE '0001-01-01'` (`UserOrganisationRepository.cs:74`), an agreement-code row from the backfill seeder's `new DateOnly(1, 1, 1)` parameter, so `-infinity` (`UserAgreementCodeBackfillSeeder.cs:151`; a row written by the column default is `0001-01-01`; both read back in C# as `DateOnly.MinValue`). Your reader's bounds are always real dates (the day before the opening Monday, the period's last day), so its `effective_from > @afterExclusive` test never selects a "since always" row of either kind, and that is correct: such a row is no change inside any period.
