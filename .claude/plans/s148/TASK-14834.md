## Plain language — why

**What an institution gets.** Some institutions give their staff a day off that no agreement requires: 5 June (Grundlovsdag) from noon, 24 December, a closure day such as the Friday after Ascension. ADR-043 D9 calls these *customary days* and says each institution records its own, dated and audited, so that withdrawing one ("from next year we no longer close on 24 December") or reinstating it later is a dated fact, never a deletion. Wave 4 built the table (`init.sql:983-1009`); nothing can fill it yet. This task gives a local administrator the routes to **list, create, withdraw and reinstate** the institution's customary days.

**What it must also get right.**
1. **Only the institution's own administrators.** The routes are `LocalAdminOrAbove` and checked against the organisation in the address, through the LocalAdmin floor, exactly as the absence-type visibility write is (`ConfigEndpoints.cs:594-598`). A LocalAdmin of STY02 who tries to write STY01's customs gets 403 and nothing is written (AC-C10b).
2. **Every write is a row, an audit row and an event, in one transaction** (ADR-018 D3, ADR-026): the `customary_days` row, a `customary_days_audit` row, a `CustomaryDayChanged` outbox event on the organisation's stream, and the audit-projection row an administrator can read. If any of them fails, none is kept (AC-C10a).
3. **No pay effect and no pay machinery.** Nothing reads customary days for pay until stage 1 (S1-9, the stage-1 reader of customs, `REF:508`, `:642`). So these writes take **no** pay-inputs lock, land **no** row on HR's correction worklist, and are **not** frozen into a month's proof: there is nothing a recorded month could be stale on (`REF:508`, `:615`). PIN-1 must stay green (AC-C10c).

**Worked example** (the clock Tuesday 10 November 2026; a LocalAdmin of STY01).
- *Create.* She records 5 June as a paid day off from 12:00, given to everyone, valid from Monday 5 January 2026. Answer 201 with the row (`version` 1, `validTo` null). Written in one transaction: the row (`org_id` STY01, `recurring_month` 6, `recurring_day` 5, `kind` `PAID_DAY_OFF`, `part` `FROM_TIME`, `part_from_time` 12:00, `paid_mode` `GIVEN_TO_ALL`, `valid_from` 2026-01-05); one `customary_days_audit` row `CREATED` (version null → 1); one `CustomaryDayChanged` event, `Action` `CREATED`, on stream `customary-days-STY01`; one audit-projection row targeting STY01.
- *Withdraw.* From Monday 2 November 2026 the institution stops the custom. She withdraws it with `validTo` 2026-11-02. Answer 200, the same row at `version` 2 with `valid_to` 2026-11-02; one audit row `WITHDRAWN` (1 → 2); one event `WITHDRAWN` with `ValidTo` set.
- *Reinstate.* She reinstates it from Tuesday 10 November 2026. Answer 201 with a **new** row (`version` 1, `valid_from` 2026-11-10, the same day, kind, part and mode) whose event carries `PrecedingCustomId` = the withdrawn row (BC-4's lean). The withdrawn row keeps `valid_to` 2026-11-02, so the eight days between still read as "no custom". One audit row `REINSTATED`; one event `REINSTATED`.
- *A LocalAdmin of STY02* posting the same create to `/api/config/STY01/customary-days`: 403 `{ error: "Access denied", reason }`, nothing written.

**What does not change.** No table, column, event shape, mapper, serializer entry or catalog row (TASK-14815 made them); no pay figure; no existing route.
