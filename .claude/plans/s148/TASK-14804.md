## Plain language — why

**The problem.** A day of paid absence is worth what the person would have worked that day. Payroll credits paid absence (holiday, sickness, a care day, paid leave …) as if it were worked time, so a day that holds more paid absence than it was planned to hold creates flex hours out of nothing. Today the Skema save cannot see that happening, for three reasons:

1. it adds up only the absence cells **in the one save it is handling**, and the page sends only the cells that changed;
2. it never counts **hours worked** on the same day;
3. on a weekend it lets a sick day or paid leave count **up to 7.4 hours**, although nobody was planned to work at all.

**A worked day.** Take Wednesday 10 June 2026 for a full-time employee: the plan for the day is 7,4 hours.

- *Two saves, one day (the owner's own finding, QUAL-237).* Morning: Ferie 7,4 is saved. Afternoon, in a separate save: Sygdom 7,4. Each save checks only itself (7,4 ≤ 7,4), so both are accepted, and payroll credits **14,8 hours** for a day worth 7,4: the employee's flex rises by 7,4 hours that were never worked. The owner saw exactly this on the demo stack (`admin01`, 13 October: Ferie 7,4 + Sygdom 7,4).
- *A partly worked day (QUAL-231's residual).* The employee works 3,0 hours and goes home sick. The sick part may be at most 7,4 − 3,0 = **4,4 hours**: then the day counts 7,4, as planned. Today 7,4 hours of sickness on top of the 3,0 worked are accepted, and the day counts 10,4.
- *The other direction.* The day already holds Sygdom 7,4; later the employee types 7,4 hours of work on it. Today that is accepted; the day counts 14,8.
- *A weekend (QUAL-240).* Saturday 13 June has no planned hours. A sick day saved on it today is credited up to 7,4 hours of flex.

**The rule after this task**, in one line: *a day's paid-absence hours may not exceed the hours planned for the day minus the hours worked on it* — written `paid absence ≤ max(0, planned − worked)`. Every save that touches a day (an absence, a project entry or work time) is judged on what the day will hold **after** the save, including what was saved earlier. Each example above now gets a clear refusal (HTTP 422, "the request is understood but not allowed") instead of a silent over-credit.

**Why at the save, not in payroll.** The owner's ruling (ADR-043 D2, R-7 as widened) puts the check where the employee types: they are told at once and can correct it. The alternative, payroll quietly clipping the credit, would hide the problem from the employee and change the engine's arithmetic, which this sprint must not do: no demo payslip may change (PIN-1), and no demo day holds both absence and work. *Given up:* protection against data written by some path other than the Skema save; once the old time-registration route retires (TASK-14802, this wave) there is no such path.

**What "planned" means for now.** The planned hours are the day's ordinary norm share (§ 7 nr. 2 of the state working-time agreement: the hours the person should have worked; 7,4 for a full-timer on a weekday, 0 on a weekend). Stage 1 (S149) replaces it with a configurable "credit basis"; this limit is named and registered (R-23).

**Why it must be checked twice.** The save checks once before it opens its database transaction (cheap, gives the quick answer) and again **inside the per-employee lock**, where the answer is final: two saves arriving at the same moment could otherwise each see an empty day and together overfill it (AC-S0-7e).

## Pattern

**One operation, one date.** A handler (or the outermost unit of work) reads the business date ONCE, before it opens a
connection or transaction, and passes that value to everything the operation writes or compares — repository calls,
SQL parameters, event fields, audit payloads:

Rules:
- **The caller that owns the operation reads the date; callees accept it.** Give repository methods an OPTIONAL trailing
  `DateOnly? closeDate = null` (or `asOf`) so existing callers and direct test constructions keep compiling; the fallback

  finding. When a comment says "computed once", the code must have exactly one read — the S137 comment was true for
  three of five cells and nobody noticed for two sprints.
