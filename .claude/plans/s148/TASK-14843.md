## Plain language — why

**The problem.** Danish law (arbejdsmiljøloven § 50, BEK 606 § 3) says an employee must have "at least 11 consecutive hours of rest within each period of 24 hours". Wave 3 taught the server to read the clock times employees type in Skema, but it measures rest the wrong way: it groups work by the day it *starts* and measures only the gap between one day's last end and the next day's first start. So it misses real breaches: one shift longer than 13 hours, a day shift plus a night shift on the same day, a split day that runs more than 13 hours from first start to last end. Those days now look compliant. That is QUAL-253 (High).

**The rule the authority uses.** Arbejdstilsynet's own test (AT-meddelelse 5.01.1, unchanged 1997–2024): pick a moment inside working time, look **24 hours back**; that stretch must hold **11 unbroken hours of rest**. Checking at the end of every piece of work is enough, because the best rest in the window only shrinks while work goes on. In practice: nobody may still be working more than 13 hours after their last full rest ended.

**What this task changes.** The server's daily rest check is rewritten to that test (RR-1):
- every piece of work that ends inside the month is judged on the 24 hours before its end;
- the number reported is the **longest unbroken rest** inside those 24 hours, **shown rounded down** to one decimal (10 h 59 min shows 10.9, never "11.0 against a minimum of 11.0"); under 11 hours is a finding;
- the finding is **dated the day the work ends**, one finding per day, at the day's lowest value;
- hours are **real elapsed hours in Danish time**, so the night the clocks go forward is one hour shorter and the night they go back one hour longer; the engine is **handed** Denmark's time-zone rules by the host that starts it and never looks them up itself, so it stays a pure function (ADR-002);
- the "derogation allowed" setting (HK, PROSA) no longer excuses everything: a rest of **8 hours or more** is a warning, **below 8 hours** it is a breach (RR-2), and the warning says what StatsTid does not check;
- the server reads **two days** before the month, not one, because the day before the 1st can be only 23 hours long.

**Two worked examples that move.**
1. *Mette types Tuesday 10 March 22:00–23:59 and Wednesday 11 March 06:00–14:00.* Today the server reports a 6.0-hour rest dated Tuesday (the gap 24:00 → 06:00). After this task it reports **8.0 hours dated Wednesday**: in the 24 hours before Wednesday 14:00 the longest unbroken rest is Tuesday 14:00–22:00. Both are breaches; the number and the date change. (For an HK employee, today's 6.0 is a warning; the corrected 8.0 is still a warning, exactly on the 8-hour floor.)
2. *Jens works one 24-hour shift, Friday 6 March 12:00 → Saturday 7 March 12:00* (typed 12:00–23:59 and 00:00–12:00). Today the server sees one working day and reports **nothing**. After this task it reports **0.0 hours dated Saturday**: the 24 hours before Saturday 12:00 hold no rest at all. For an HK employee this is a **violation**, not a warning, because 0.0 is below the 8-hour floor.

**What does not change.** No pay figure (payroll never runs the rest rule, ADR-039 D5; AC-S0-3n). The weekly rest-day check (RR-4: it is also wrong, registered as QUAL-256, not rebuilt now). The 13-hour max-daily check and the 48-hour average. How a night shift typed in two halves is joined, and the "rest not checked" list. The manager rule: a manager sees nothing from a month the employee has not sent, now for both days before the month. No screen change: that is TASK-14844.

~~~markdown
**RR-1 — The rule: judge every work end on the 24 hours before it.**

- **The rule in plain words.** For each piece of work that ends inside the month, look back 24 hours from its end. If the longest unbroken rest inside those 24 hours is under 11 hours, that is a finding.
- **Why this form and not "chains spanning at most 13 hours".** The two give identical verdicts (verification V-75). The chain form needs to know where the chain began, which can be any number of days back. The look-back form needs 24 hours.
- **Boundaries.** Exactly 11.0 hours passes ("mindst 11"). Rest outside the window does not count; rest partly inside counts for its inside part only. Short rests are never added together.
- **The limit is derived, not a second setting.** The 13 hours is 24 minus `MinimumRestHours`; the check does not read `MaxDailyHours`.
- **Elapsed hours.** All arithmetic is in elapsed time in Europe/Copenhagen, never wall-clock difference and never the host's or browser's own zone.
  - A typed time in the hour that occurs twice in October is read as the later instant for an end and the earlier for a start.
  - A typed time in the hour that does not exist in March is read as the changeover instant.
  - Both choices err toward reporting. They are my defaults; the sources say only that the rule is in hours.
- **Dating and month.** A finding is dated the calendar day its work ends and belongs to that day's month. This replaces "a rest is judged in the month in which it ends" (BC-4) and removes the oddity of a finding dated 31 March owned by April.
- **One finding per day.** If several work ends on one day fail, the day gets one finding with the lowest value.
- **The value.** The longest unbroken rest inside the 24 hours before the judged work end, against the threshold 11. `DAILY_REST` keeps its shape; the message names the judged time.
- **Data edge.**
  - Time with no registered work is rest. That is the directive's own definition, and the existing not-checked list (R-22) already marks days whose hours are not all on the clock.
  - The read starts at `monthStart − 2`, not `− 1`. The day before the month can be 23 hours long (1 April 2030 is the next case), so 24 elapsed hours can reach 60 minutes into the day before that.
  - The manager-visibility rule and its marker from the 5a adjudication apply unchanged to both days.
- **Work running past the month's last minute** is judged as the month sees it, and again in full in the next month.
- **Voluntary work.** A stint that is wholly voluntary is left out of the check: it is neither judged nor counted as work in someone else's window (`SYSTEM_TARGET.md:300`). The cut-at-read-edge rule from the 5a adjudication stays. Nothing writes this flag today.
- **A typed end of 23:59** is read as the end of the day, since Skema cannot hold 24:00 and the midnight join already counts that minute as work. If an existing pinned fact contradicts this, the task stops and reports.
- **Given up:**
  - The number is no longer always "the gap between two shifts". Example: 22:00–23:59, then 06:00–14:00. Today it shows 6.0; the corrected check shows 8.0, the rest from 14:00 to 22:00 inside the window.
  - A chain already over the limit before a month edge appears in both months, each with its own true value.
- **Unchanged:** the stint builder and midnight join, the not-checked list, and the max-daily and 48-hour checks. The max-daily hours sum stays as a backstop for hours off the clock.

**RR-2 — Derogation: add the 8-hour floor now; register the rest.**

- **Today.** The flag turns any short rest into a warning (`CheckDailyRest`), so a 24-hour shift for an HK employee is only a warning.
- **Build now.** With the flag on, a finding is a warning only when its value is at least 8.0 hours; below that it is a violation.
  - This is exactly the § 12 cut: a window holding 8 unbroken hours is the same as a 16-hour span.
  - The 8 is a named constant citing BEK 606 § 19 and the state agreement § 12. It is the order's number, identical in OK21 and OK26, so it is not an agreement setting.
- **The warning's text must say what is not checked:** that a cut is allowed at most once per 7 days, and only with equal compensatory rest straight after, and that StatsTid checks neither.
- **Register, not built (QUAL-254):** the once or twice per 7 days count, "not two days in a row", the compensatory hours (Jaeger para. 97), postponement, and a record of who decided the cut.
- **Why not now.** Three of these rest on silent questions (a), (b) and (d). They also need a decision record and an HR hand-off that nobody has designed.
~~~
