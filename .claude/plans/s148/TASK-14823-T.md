## Plain language — why

**What TASK-14823 changes.** A configuration change dated into the past (a new agreement version, a position override, an institution's local profile) now puts every already-paid month it reaches on HR's correction worklist, and flags every settled holiday year it reaches, with a new reason, `CONFIG_PUBLISHED`. And each of the six configuration writes now takes the system-wide "pay inputs" lock as its very first step, so it takes turns with payroll's recordings.

**What you prove.**
1. **The right months, no more, no fewer.** An OK24 publish dated Monday 5 January 2026 lists January to April 2026 and not May (AC-C2g); a position override lists only the people who held that position on a day it applies, and a local profile only the people who belonged to that institution then, by the dated organisation (AC-C2f); a match that ends on Sunday 29 March lists March and not April (AC-C2j); a publish before a paid month leaves that month's lines alone and lands a worklist row with its event and audit row (AC-C2c, the hand-off half).
2. **Settled holiday years are flagged, never revalued** (AC-C2i).
3. **The new reason is known and never blocks a recalculation** (AC-C2h, a Unit fact).
4. **Each writer waits for the lock first and holds nothing else while it waits** (AC-PIL-e row (2)). "Holds nothing else" matters: a writer that held some other lock while waiting could close a circle with another request and stall the system.
5. **How long a big publish holds the lock** (a measurement, recorded, not asserted against a limit): a slow figure becomes a performance-register row.
6. **The local-profile save refuses a predecessor that changed while it waited** (AC-C2u). TASK-14823 fixes a real gap: a save that waited for the lock while another administrator replaced the same profile would otherwise pass its version check and record an audit row about the wrong row. Your fact makes that happen on purpose and checks the save answers "precondition failed" (412) and writes nothing; a deliberate break of the fix (M-49) proves the fact can fail.
7. **One change lists a month once** (AC-C2t), even when an employee matches the change in two separate stretches that both reach that month.

**Why none of this is timing-dependent.** The lock facts let the test decide the order: the test holds the lock first, starts the request, waits until PostgreSQL itself shows that request waiting on that lock, checks what the waiting request holds, then releases. Never a sleep. The hand-off facts have no race at all.

Expected values come from the plan and the briefs, never from what the code returns.
