## Plain language — why

**The problem.** An agreement configuration (the central rules for, say, HK under OK26: the weekly norm, the flex cap, the supplement rates) and a position override (a variation of those rules for one position) carry no date today. The database knows when a version was *published*, not from which day it *applies*. So the system cannot answer "which rules applied on Friday 30 October?", and payroll could not pay October and November under different versions even if it read the database. (It does not yet: it prices on rules compiled into the code until wave 5's engine switch.) Agreements are often signed after they take effect, so the date a GlobalAdmin needs is often in the past (ADR-043 D12).

**What this task changes.**
- **Every publish and every override change carries an "applies from" date.** Left empty, it means today in Danish time, never the empty-date trap `0001-01-01`. It may lie in the past, never in the future; a future date is refused with today as the suggestion. A change to a field the weekly calculation reads week by week (the weekly norm, and any other field on a closed list a read-only trace establishes before this task starts) must start on a Monday until stage 1 removes the weekly basis: a known, registered limit. A brand-new agreement's first version may start on any weekday, because nothing was in force before it, so no week is judged under two rules.
- **Every published version is kept, and one read answers "which version was in force on day X?"**: the published version with the latest date on or before X. "Archived" now means "replaced from a later date", not "never applies". A draft that was thrown away without being published never counts, even though it is stored as ARCHIVED; that is why the read looks at the "published" stamp, not at the status. Two publishes dated the same day: the one that took effect last wins, and its stamp is taken at the moment it takes effect, so the answer never depends on which transaction happened to start first. Two or more administrators writing the same agreement (or the same position's override) at once are made to take turns, so "never dated before the version it replaces" holds however many write together.
- **A position override keeps its history.** The editable row stays; each change also writes a dated row into a history table, so "was the override active on Friday 6 November?" still has an answer after it was switched off from Monday 9 November.
- **Archiving the active configuration is refused** ("publish a successor"): under dated versions it would silently bring an older version back into force.
- **Payroll's planner learns the dates.** A month in which a new version starts is split at that day, so that from wave 5 each part is paid under its own version. This is QUAL-182's planner half. The planner gets the ability now, but the payroll service is handed the two tables only in wave 5 (TASK-14825), so **no payroll run splits a month in wave 4**. When a version starts on the very day an employee changes agreement or position, that day is already a split, and the record keeps naming the change of agreement or position, the change that actually happened to that person.
- **The startup seeder retires.** It could only ever write unpublished rows, which the new read ignores; `init.sql`'s rows are the one seed.

**A worked example.** On Wednesday 11 November 2026 a GlobalAdmin learns that the HK / OK26 weekly norm is 32 hours from Monday 2 November. She publishes the new version with "applies from 2 November". From then on the read answers 37 hours for Friday 30 October and 32 hours for Monday 2 November, and payroll's planner, once TASK-14825 hands it the tables in wave 5, splits November at the 2nd. Had she typed Wednesday 4 November, the server would refuse and offer Monday 2 or Monday 9 November; had she typed Monday 16 November, a future date, it would refuse and offer today. **What does not change yet:** no payslip moves. Payroll still prices every day on the compiled rules until wave 5 (`REF:414`), and the hand-off that puts the already-paid months a past date reaches on HR's worklist is TASK-14823's (wave 5). PIN-1 holds: every seeded version applies from `0001-01-01`, so no demo month gains a split.

~~~markdown
> **Amended by [ADR-018](ADR-018-transactional-outbox-and-row-version-optimistic-concurrency.md) (S22):** the window math below (predecessor closed at `effective_from - 1`, end-inclusive) produced invalid history windows on same-day re-saves. **FAIL-007 — the planner's cause names the transition that ends a segment** (`:13-30`; omitted: the header table):
~~~

~~~markdown
## Summary
`PeriodPlanner.Plan` gives segment 0 and every MIDDLE segment the cause of the boundary that
introduces the NEXT segment; only the FINAL segment carries the cause of the boundary that started
it (see the "2 + 3. Build contiguous segments" comment block in `PeriodPlanner.cs`). Two-segment
plans hide this — both segments get the same cause — so the convention is easy to invert the first
time a THREE-segment plan is asserted.

## How it surfaced
TASK-13702's hire-and-leave test (NOT_EMPLOYED / EMPLOYED / NOT_EMPLOYED) asserted
`EmploymentStarted` on the employed middle segment and failed: by the convention the middle
segment carries `EmploymentEnded` (the transition that ends it). The test was corrected; the
planner was NOT changed — manifests already persist this shape, and a "fix" would silently
re-label every stored manifest on replay.

## Lesson
For plans with 3 or more segments, assert causes per the convention (leading edge for the last
segment, "what ends me" for the others). Do not "fix" the planner to match intuition. When a reader
needs the leading-edge cause of a middle segment, derive it from the PREVIOUS segment's cause.
~~~

**ADR-041 — Copenhagen business day: the decision, the rule, the testing consequence** (`:11-13`, `:34-43`, `:64-76`; omitted: what was wrong, why, consequences, what it does not decide):

~~~markdown
## The decision, in one sentence

**Every business date in StatsTid is the calendar day in Europe/Copenhagen; every instant stays UTC.**

## The rule

**Business dates MOVE to the Copenhagen day.** `effective_from` / `effective_to`, any validator asking "is this in the
past/future?", any stored effective-date stamp, any date offered to a user as a default. These are calendar facts about
Danish employment.

**Instants STAY UTC.** `created_at`, `updated_at`, audit timestamps, outbox ordering, JWT expiry. These are moments in time.
**Converting an instant to a calendar day and back corrupts ordering**, which would compromise the auditability invariant —
so mistaking an instant for a date is the single most damaging way to apply this ADR wrongly.


## Testing consequence — read this before writing a date test

**`WithFixedToday(DateOnly)` pins UTC midnight, the one instant where both calendars always agree.** A test built on it
passes under a correct *and* a broken implementation. It remains correct for tests whose subject is not the calendar.

A test whose subject **is** the business day must pin an exact instant where the calendars disagree, via
`WithFixedInstant(DateTimeOffset)` and the shared anchors in `BoundaryInstants` — and must assert **literal** expected
values. *A test that computes its expectation by calling the helper under test proves only that the helper agrees with
itself*; S142 found three such tests and deleted the shape.

**On a developer machine in Denmark, a frontend date test is vacuous unless it forces a non-Danish zone**, because the
browser-local day and the Danish day are the same day there. Force the zone, and assert that the forcing worked.
~~~
