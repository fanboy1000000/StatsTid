## Plain language — why

**What TASK-14830 changes.** Each month's stored proof (its frozen inputs) now holds the agreement rules it was paid on, by value, one copy per stretch of days. A replay (re-running a month to prove its figures) sends exactly those stored rules and never looks them up again. The old fingerprint of the rules, its check and its refusal (`replay-config-changed`) are gone.

**What you prove.**
1. **A later change to the rules does not change a replay** (AC-C3a). A month is calculated, then an administrator publishes a different version of its rules dated back into it, then the month is replayed: the replay must give back exactly the original. The opposite would mean a stored proof silently moves when someone edits a rule.
2. **An older proof is refused by name, never re-run on today's rules** (AC-C3b), whichever of the two places the proof is read from (the projection row, or the event when the row is gone).
3. **No rule is lost in storage** (the round-trip pin). Every one of the 41 settings of an agreement rule set survives being written into the proof and read back, on both storage paths. The real risk the adjudicator named: a setting that the serializer silently drops would replay a different month (the batch-C adjudication, § 3).
4. **The fingerprint's own tests retire with it**, and the one test in their file that guards the arithmetic's version name moves to a file of its own.

Expected values come from the plan and the briefs, never from what the code returns.
