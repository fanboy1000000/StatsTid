## Plain language — why

**The problem: a race nobody sees.** Payroll works in two steps. First it calculates a month, outside any lock. A moment later it records the result in one transaction. HR's corrections run in between. A correction dated back into that month looks for months already recorded, to put them on HR's worklist. If it commits in the moment between payroll's two steps, neither side sees the other. The correction finds no record yet and lists nothing. Payroll then records the month on the values from before the correction. The month is paid on stale inputs, and nobody is told. The owner's answer "nothing silent" (Q-2 = (a)) rules that out. S138's profile and agreement-code corrections have had this gap since they were built. Three older writers lead into it too: the employment start date, the employment end date, and closing a person's profile.

**The fix has two halves. This task builds the Backend half.**
- **One shared lock, "pay inputs".** Every writer that dates a pay input into the past takes it *exclusive*, as the very first thing in its transaction. Payroll's export and correction take it *shared* (TASK-14831, wave 5). Many payroll recordings can run side by side, but a correction waits for the ones in progress, and new recordings wait for the correction.
- **Whichever comes first, the other sees it.** If the correction commits first, payroll's own check (wave 5) refuses with "calculate again". If the recording commits first, the correction sees the record and lists the month on the worklist.
- **This task:** creates the lock, and puts it first in the seven Backend writers that need it in wave 4.
- **The two employment-date writers and the settlement reversal now tell HR**, as an agreement-code correction already does. When they move an employment date back into a paid month or a settled holiday year, the month or year lands on the worklist under a new reason, `EMPLOYMENT_WINDOW_CHANGE`.
- **The start-date write gains its own event**, so its worklist row can point at what caused it.
- **Closing a profile** lists the current month on the worklist when payroll has already recorded it. *Why:* payroll may record a month before it ends.
- **Nobody can change their own pay inputs.** An HR user cannot change their own agreement code, hours fraction, position or category. Only an actual change is refused. A save that changes nothing, such as correcting one's own e-mail through the edit drawer, which re-sends every field, still answers 200.
- **Nobody can close their own profile either.** Closing a profile ends, from today, the record of the person's hours fraction, position and category, which are pay inputs. The adjudication found that door open and closed it here, because this task already edits that code (the owner's A-18 rule); the owner is told and may strike it.

**Worked example.** *An employee's employment started Sunday 15 March 2026, and March 2026 has been sent to payroll. HR learns the real first day was Monday 2 March and corrects it.*
- Today the change is saved and nobody is told: March stays paid as if 2–14 March were not employed.
- After this task, the correction waits for any payroll recording in progress, then saves. In the same transaction it puts March on HR's worklist with the reason "employment-window change", dated 2 March, pointing at the new start-date event.
- If payroll was recording March at that moment, one of the two waits for the other, and the second always sees the first.

**What it costs (accepted, B-7).** Corrections to pay inputs now take turns with each other across the whole system. Every save from HR's edit drawer takes the lock, because the drawer always sends the organisation and the agreement code. While such a save waits for another lock, payroll recordings wait behind it. TASK-14831 measures the longest such waits.

**What does not change.** No screen, no route shape, no pay figure. The employment-date routes' existing guards stay as they are: the self-target 403, the re-hire 409, the inverted-window 422, the strand and settlement guards. The user create, the customary-days writers and the HR-facts writers take no lock in S148. PIN-1 holds: the demo runs no correction during a recording.

*In plain words.* Payroll works in two steps. It calculates a month outside any lock, then records the result in one transaction (the "export"; `/recalculate`'s correction works the same way). A change dated back into that month looks, in its own transaction, for months already recorded and lists them on HR's worklist.
