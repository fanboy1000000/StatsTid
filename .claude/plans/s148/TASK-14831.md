## Plain language — why

**The problem.** Payroll works in two steps: it **calculates** a month (outside any lock, over a few seconds), then **records** it in one database transaction (the export; `/recalculate`, the correction, works the same way). A change dated back into that month (a new agreement version, a position override, an institution's local profile, a transfer, a profile or employment-date correction) looks, in its own transaction, for months already recorded and puts them on HR's worklist. If the change commits in the moment between payroll's two steps, neither side sees the other: the change finds no record to list, and payroll records the month on the values from before the change. The month is paid on stale inputs and nobody is told (the refinement's PIL section, `REF:569`).

**The fix has two halves.** TASK-14821 and 14823 made every such change take the system-wide "pay inputs" lock exclusively, as its first step. **This task is the payroll half:** the export and the correction take the same lock in its *shared* mode as their first step (many recordings may run together; a change waits for them, and they wait for a change), and, inside their turn, **calculate the month's inputs again and compare them with what they calculated with**. With both halves, either order is safe: a change that commits first is caught by payroll's check, which refuses "calculate the month again"; a recording that commits first is seen by the change, which lists the month.

**Worked example 1 — the change first.** Mette (HK, STY01) has March 2026 approved. At 10:00:00 payroll calculates March on HK OK24's 37 hours. At 10:00:01 a global administrator publishes 32 hours dated Monday 2 February 2026; the publish looks for recorded months and finds no March record yet. Payroll then asks for its turn: the publish holds the lock, so payroll waits. The publish commits; payroll gets its turn, derives March's inputs again, finds 32 hours where it calculated with 37, and answers **409 `payroll-inputs-changed`** naming March. Nothing is recorded. The retry calculates on 32 hours and records March. *Today:* March is recorded on 37 hours and nobody is told.

**Worked example 2 — the change inside the month (B-2).** The same, but the publish is dated **Monday 16 March 2026**. A check that looked up "the version in force on 1 March" would find the old one and pass; March would be recorded on 37 hours for 16–31 March. The fresh derivation instead cuts March at the 16th, which the frozen inputs do not: a difference, 409. The retry pays 1–15 March on 37 hours and 16–31 March on 32 (AC-PIL-a2).

**Worked example 3 — the recording first.** Payroll gets its turn first and records March on 37 hours. The publish waits, then gets its turn, finds March's record and lists March on the worklist with the configuration trigger; a correction pays the new version (AC-PIL-b).

**What does not change.** What a calculation computes; the order and answers of every existing refusal; the flex recheck; the approval re-checks; every demo payslip (PIN-1: no change commits during a pin run, so every check passes).

### The refinement (rev 5.1), verbatim

PIL's plain words (`REF:569`), where the two writers live (`:572-573`), the shared mode (`:588`), the export's and the correction's lock order and why no cycle forms (`:590-591`, `:600`), the deadlock rule and READ COMMITTED (`:601-602`), the re-derivation, the refusal and the correction (`:604-612`), the contention given up (`:616`), AC-PIL-f (`:624`), the order of the wave (`:786`), the T24 row (`:822`), and the two risk bullets on contention and order (`:878-879`):

*In plain words.* Payroll works in two steps. It calculates a month outside any lock, then records the result in one transaction (the "export"; `/recalculate`'s correction works the same way). A change dated back into that month looks, in its own transaction, for months already recorded and lists them on HR's worklist. If the change commits in the moment between payroll's two steps, neither side sees the other: the change finds no record yet and lists nothing, and payroll then records the month on the values from before the change. That month is paid on stale inputs and nobody is told, the one silent path left under Q-2 = (a) ("nothing silent"). S138's corrections of an employee's profile and agreement code have had this gap since they were built; S148's dated configurations and transfers would add more ways into it, and so do three older writers the cycle-3 review named: the employment start and end dates (which decide which days count as employed at all, B-3) and closing an employee's profile (B-4). The fix has two halves: the change and the recording take turns on one lock, and payroll checks inside its turn, day by day, that what it calculated with is still in force (B-2). With both, either order is safe: a change that commits first is caught by payroll's check, which refuses with "calculate again"; a recording that commits first is seen by the change, which lists the month. The precedent is S145's fix for a reopen racing an export (HRP-001b, `docs/operations/hr-follow-up-process-register.md:98`; the export's under-lock recheck of the previous month, `PayrollExportService.cs:586-599`) and S146's flex recheck (`PayrollExportService.cs:277-286`).

- **The export.** `ExportAsync` opens one transaction (`PayrollExportService.cs:262-268`); its first statement is the per-employee ledger lock (`:270-275`), then the flex recheck under that lock (`:277-286`), the previous month's approval `FOR SHARE` (`:288-298`) and this month's `FOR UPDATE` (`:304`, the query `:490-497`). Payroll locks "advisory, then approval rows, then records" (`PayrollExportRecordRepository.cs:174`). The calculation and its manifest come before the export, outside the transaction (`Payroll/Program.cs:343-358`), and every export-time refusal leaves that manifest in place (`:472-482`, `:483-492`).
- **The correction.** `RetroactiveCorrectionService` has the calculation's frozen inputs in hand before its transaction (`RetroactiveCorrectionService.cs:338-343`), opens it and takes the same ledger lock first (`:363-371`), then its own flex recheck (`:373-382`). These two are the only writers of `payroll_export_records` (`PayrollExportRecordRepository.cs:154-158`).
- **Shared, as the first statement of the transaction,** in the export (`ExportAsync`) and the correction (`RetroactiveCorrectionService`), before the ledger lock (T24). Many exports and corrections hold it at once; a writer waits until those in progress commit, and new ones queue behind the writer.
  - Export: pay inputs (shared) → `payroll-ledger-` → the rechecks (reads) → previous month's approval `FOR SHARE` → this month's approval `FOR UPDATE` → the record.
  - Correction: pay inputs (shared) → `payroll-ledger-` → the rechecks → the record `FOR UPDATE`.
  - *Why no wait cycle can form:* a transaction waiting for the pay-inputs lock holds nothing yet, and every transaction that holds it took it before anything else.
- **The deadlock rule, per request (B-8).** The order above is per transaction; a request can use more than one connection, so every task brief that touches one of these routes carries three sentences: **(1)** a request waiting for the pay-inputs lock holds no lock on any connection (nothing taken before it, on this connection or another); **(2)** the lock is taken only at a route's own transaction head (or a service's that owns its transaction, as the reversal service does), never inside a repository helper or a shared writer (the lifecycle writer already leaves locks to its callers, `EmploymentEndDateLifecycleWriter.cs:37-42`); **(3)** a read on a separate connection never takes it, not even shared. Sentence 3 is load-bearing: the profile route's revaluation reads the dated profile on its own connection (`EmployeeProfileEndpoints.cs:1780`, `:1785-1786`) while its request holds the lock exclusive; that read taking the lock shared would wait for its own request forever.
- **READ COMMITTED, explicitly, in every transaction that takes it.** Under READ COMMITTED each statement sees what was committed before it started, so the change's worklist read, made after its turn begins, sees a recording that committed while it waited. Under REPEATABLE READ the transaction's view is fixed when its first statement starts, which is the lock request itself, before the wait, so that recording would stay invisible: the lesson S78 recorded on the user edit (`AdminEndpoints.cs:1770-1773`). The local-profile write therefore moves from REPEATABLE READ to READ COMMITTED (`ConfigEndpoints.cs:264`). Its repository's guarantees still hold there: the row lock and the partial unique index serialize two writers of one profile (`LocalAgreementProfileRepository.cs:191-194`, `:310-336`), and the exclusive pay-inputs lock serializes every writer anyway. Transactions that use the driver's default today state READ COMMITTED explicitly, as S78 R5 asks (`AdminEndpoints.cs:1770`): among them the profile PUT (`EmployeeProfileEndpoints.cs:385`) and the profile close (`:1218`). - **Inside its turn, payroll re-derives its inputs and compares the whole derivation (B-2).** After the pay-inputs lock is granted and beside the flex recheck (before any row lock), the export and the correction run again the derivation that produced their frozen inputs (C-3, C-13), over the same span the calculation read (the month's days and the context days around them, `PlanInputs.cs:37-41`), and compare **the whole result** with the frozen one:

  - **the settlement cutoff** (`SettlementCutoff`, `PlanInputs.cs:85`; why it is frozen, `:42-49`), which an end-date change moves.

  Any difference refuses. *Why the whole derivation and not one value per run:* rev 3 compared each run's config as resolved for the run, so a version dated Monday 16 March, committing first inside a March that had been calculated as one run on the old version, could be looked up "as of the run's start" (1 March), find the old version and compare equal, and March would be recorded on the old values for 16–31 March with no worklist row (the change had found no record to list). Comparing the runs' edges catches the new cut at the 16th. Likewise an employment start moved earlier changes the EMPLOYED set, and an end date moved changes the cutoff, though no per-day value of an already-employed day changes. **A day the fresh derivation cannot cover is a difference**, never a pass: a day left with no profile (the profile close) is the shape evaluation refuses as `employment_record_gap` (`PeriodCalculationService.cs:2206-2207`). *By value* means by the frozen JSON form both sides serialize to, because `AgreementRuleConfig` is a class compared by reference (`AgreementRuleConfig.cs:3`). While the shared lock is held no writer of a pay input can commit, so the comparison sees a stable state. **ADR-034 D1–D5 — the payroll-export lock and the record** (`docs/knowledge-base/decisions/ADR-034-payroll-export-lock.md:19-39`, `:43-44`; omitted: the header and Context `:1-16`, the S147 chain-walk note `:41`, Consequences and the S147 amendment `:46-155`):

### D1 — One Payroll-owned record = lock + idempotency + corrections-manifest
A new `payroll_export_records` table (Payroll context; regular monthly time, NOT settlement): `export_id` PK, nullable `period_id`, `employee_id`, `year`, `month`, `exported_at`, `original_lines JSONB` (immutable manifest as first exported), `current_effective_lines JSONB` (the evolving correction baseline — D5), `content_hash`, `source`; **UNIQUE `(employee_id, year, month)`**. The row's existence = "this month is sent to payroll / locked." The same row serves three jobs: the lock (existence), idempotency (the unique key + content_hash), and the corrections manifest (the lines).

### D2 — Atomic export; the lock is a "durable handoff record", not "delivered" (OQ-1/OQ-5)
`PayrollExportService.ExportAsync` is refactored to inject `IOutboxEnqueue` + the audit deps and run ONE `(conn, tx)`: `GuardSettlementLineDelivery` FIRST (fail-closed ordering preserved) → group lines by `(employee_id, year, month)` (D3) → per group, `FOR UPDATE`-re-lock the approval row + re-assert `status='APPROVED'` (D4 race) → INSERT `payroll_export_records` → emit `PayrollExportGenerated` to **`outbox_events`** (the ADR-018 canonical event outbox, NOT the `outbox_messages` delivery envelope) → write the ADR-026 audit row → COMMIT. The lock is set at COMMIT (OQ-1 "export committed"). **There is NO background dispatcher for `outbox_messages WHERE destination='payroll'`** (the only poller is `destination='external'`), so the actual mock-SLS send stays the EXISTING synchronous best-effort HTTP POST + status update, POST-commit; the lock does NOT depend on delivery. The event is REUSED from the vestigial `PayrollExportGenerated` (reshaped payload — replay-safe, it was never emitted; a 2nd live payroll-export event would be rot), now a real audited event (Infrastructure `IAuditProjectionMapper` + Payroll DI + catalog row; the catalog "defined-but-unemitted TBD" resolved).

*(Amended S147, see "Amendment 2026-11-04" below: an export is one (employee, year, month), so there is no grouping step; the approval period is required, so the `FOR UPDATE` re-assert of APPROVED, with the four-eyes checkpoint, runs on every export; and it first checks that the period is the exported employee's whole calendar month.)*

### D3 — Idempotency by content (OQ-6); multi-period exports
On the UNIQUE `(employee_id, year, month)` conflict: **same content_hash → no-op** (return the existing export, no duplicate record/event/envelope); **different content_hash → a discriminated 422/409** "already exported for {month}; use a correction." The raw `/export`+`/export-period` (which bypass approval and flatten multiple periods/months into one call) GROUP lines by `(employee_id, year, month)` and write one record per tuple, all-or-nothing; a line spanning calendar months is rejected. A ZERO-line month writes NO record (nothing handed off → stays reopenable). *(Amended S147, see "Amendment 2026-11-04": the raw routes are retired and an export is one (employee, year, month), so nothing is grouped; a month before the ledger start with no lines and no record writes no record, by this rule.)*

**D3 amendment 2026-10-24 (S146 TASK-14605, merged `8f366802`; rulings R-3, R-16, R-47; QUAL-188; the owner informed, OQ-4):** on the two *calculating* routes (`/calculate-and-export`, `/recalculate`) the month's record also carries the flex balance the month opened and closed at, and where the opening came from (`opening_balance`, `closing_balance`, `balance_provenance` ∈ `LEDGER_START | CHAINED | REHIRE`). The next month opens from that closing; nobody sends an opening any more. Three consequences, each following D3's own reason ("nothing handed off → stays reopenable"):
1. **An employed month with zero lines writes its record** (lines empty, balances set), because its closing balance *is* handed off to the next month. It is therefore locked like any exported month (D5). A month with no EMPLOYED day settles no flex and still writes no record. The raw routes carry no balance and keep D3 unchanged. *(S147: the raw routes are retired. 2. **The calculating routes accept only one whole calendar month** (400 `payroll-period-not-a-month`, before any read), because the record's closing is the *month's* closing (R-47). A legacy `WEEKLY` approval can no longer be sent through `/calculate-and-export`; it was already unusable there (the second week answered 409 and locked the month).
3. **The same-hash no-op on the calculating routes also compares the triple** (opening, provenance, closing), so a re-run whose lines match but whose balance moved answers 409 instead of silently keeping a stale balance (R-16).

So a re-run of a month WITH lines always has a different hash and answers the "use a correction" 409; the same-hash no-op fires only for an employed zero-line month. ### D4 — Cross-context READ contract; the export↔reopen race
The Backend reopen **READS** `payroll_export_records` (never writes it — the Payroll context owns it; this honors the ADR-033 precedent of Backend-owns-`approval_periods` / Payroll-owns-the-export-artifact, with a read-only cross-context contract rather than a cross-context write or an eventual-consistency projection). The export↔reopen race is closed by BOTH sides taking the approval row's `FOR UPDATE` lock: the export re-asserts APPROVED under it before inserting the record; the reopen, INSIDE its advisory-locked transaction (after `AcquireTreeLockForEmployeeAsync`, before the conditional status UPDATE), takes the same row lock then reads the lock — so an export-commit and a reopen cannot interleave on the same period.

**D4 note 2026-10-24 (S146 TASK-14605, merged `8f366802`; rulings R-2, R-15, R-5):** the export transaction (all three export routes [one since S147; see "Amendment 2026-11-04"]) and the correction transaction now take a per-employee advisory lock, `pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtext('payroll-ledger-' || employee))`, as their **first** statement (all keys in one statement, ordered), then the approval rows, then the record. The Backend reopen's leader arm takes its org advisory locks, then the approval row; its employee arm takes only the approval row. The keys are distinct by name (`payroll-ledger-` vs the `employee-` and org keys). `hashtext` is 32-bit, so a collision is possible. A single collision is harmless: it only serializes two unrelated transactions, and no wait cycle can form, because every writer takes its advisory keys before any row lock (the reopen's leader arm first does a plain read, then takes its `reporting-org-` and `unit-org-` advisory keys before its approval-row lock; its employee arm takes no advisory key and starts with the approval-row lock). A cycle among advisory locks alone would need two simultaneous collisions in opposite orders; Postgres's deadlock detector would then cancel one transaction, which is safe because both writers are transactional (S146 Step 7a, R-61(a), corrected wording). The ledger lock serializes the export, the correction and the re-derivation of the opening under the lock; a correction of M therefore derives its opening from M−1's recorded closing, and M−1 is exported and cannot be reopened (D5). This closes QUAL-201 by construction. Residual (S146 Step 7a, R-57): the race still exists for the ledger-start month and for any month before it, because a raw-recorded month corrected with its flex untracked (R-14 case 3b) derives nothing from M−1. **PAT-015 — the rule** (`docs/knowledge-base/patterns/PAT-015-advisory-lock-read-committed-vs-memoized-authority.md:13-27`; omitted: the header, the collision with snapshot-memoized authority, the known sites, the checklist, the S127 addition):
~~~markdown
## The rule

**A transaction whose first statement is a blocking advisory-lock acquire must pin
`IsolationLevel.ReadCommitted` explicitly — never the default overload.**

`pg_advisory_xact_lock` blocks until granted. Under REPEATABLE READ the transaction snapshot is taken
**before** the lock is granted, so the winner's commit — *the exact thing the loser was waiting for* —
stays invisible, and the loser proceeds on pre-lock state. The lock serializes execution while the
snapshot silently un-serializes the data, which is the worst of both.

Concretely in S127: a losing sender would fail to see the winner's committed period row, take the
*create* arm instead of the *transition* arm, and collide on the unique constraint — while every
observable outcome (a 409, the same row/audit/event counts) looked identical to the correct path. That
is why the acceptance criterion for it needs a protocol-level test asserting *the loser's post-lock read
returns the row*, not merely that a 409 came back.
~~~
