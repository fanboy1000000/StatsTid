## Plain language — why

These are the tests that define "done" for Stage B, written from the specification before the code exists. They prove the following:
- a month with a mid-month change in hours, position or agreement is now paid, each part under its own codes, with the overtime arithmetic the owner ruled (Q2: per working day; Q3: the week is paid by the month holding its Sunday);
- such a month can be **replayed** later from its stored record and gives the identical result, even after the live data changed (the auditability proof path; R-54);
- the one case that stays manual (an agreement change in the middle of a week) is refused the same way everywhere;
- a timeline line that falls in no employed part of the month fails loudly instead of being filed under the wrong part (R-56);
- the raw export routes refuse split months with their own reason;
- the HR worklist agrees with payroll about which months are blocked, for an employee employed all month. Where it cannot agree (a hire or a leaver inside the month), two tests pin the known gap, QUAL-210, so a future fix turns them red on purpose (R-55).

A second spawn later breaks the code on purpose, one behaviour at a time, to show each Unit test can fail.
