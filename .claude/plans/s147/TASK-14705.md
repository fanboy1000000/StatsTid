## Plain language — why

Overtime has a small side workflow: a leader records an overtime budget for an employee (a "pre-approval"), someone approves or rejects it, and later the hours are paid out or turned into time off. None of these steps asks whether the leader is acting on **themselves**, so a leader whose scope covers their own unit can approve and pay out their own overtime alone. The owner ruled (Q-A = (a)) to close the self part now: approve, reject and compensate refuse when the employee is the actor. Requesting overtime for oneself stays allowed, because a request decides nothing and someone else then approves it. The rule that the **creator** of a pre-approval may not approve it needs a new column and waits for S148's redesign (ADR-043 F-2).
